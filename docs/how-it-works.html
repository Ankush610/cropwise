Crop Recommendation System · team guide · Nov 6, 2026

How Fasal Salah works

A farmer gives their soil card values, district and sowing month. The system says which crops fit, ranks them by the money they can make, explains why, and lets the farmer ask follow-up questions in Hindi or English.

The flowDatasetsCrop modelPrice model RankingSHAPAgentAPIWeb appLimits
In one breath
InputN, P, K, pH (kg/ha, from the Soil Health Card), district, sowing month
FitA Random Forest says which crops suit this soil, weather and season
MoneyYield × forecast price − cost decides between crops that fit about equally
WhySHAP shows which inputs pushed each crop up or down
TalkAn LLM chats with the farmer, but only repeats numbers the tools gave it

The one rule to rememberThe ML models decide. The LLM only explains. Every crop, price, yield or rainfall number the chat says comes from a tool result.

Big picture

Two halves: build once, answer every time

Some work runs once, ahead of time in the notebooks: clean the data, train the models, save files. The app then loads those files and answers each farmer in a second or two.

Model 1 · the decider

The crop suitability model

This is the model that actually picks crops. It learns from what real districts sow: if districts with soil and weather like yours put half their rabi farmland under wheat, wheat gets a suitability near 0.5.

Season model (in the app since Nov 4)

models/crop_model.joblib trained in notebook 11
AlgorithmRandom Forest, 500 trees, min 3 samples per leaf, seed 42
Trained on539 districts × 3 seasons ≈ 1,400 rows; target = DES area by season
OutputShare of that season's farmland similar districts give each of 27 crops
TrickEach district-crop row weighted by its area share, so the forest's probability ≈ area share
The 12 inputs (soil, weather, season):
NPKpH temperature (year)humidityrainfall (year) kharif rainrabi rainkharif temprabi temp season
Season comes from the sowing month: Jun–Sep kharif, Oct–Jan rabi, Feb–May summer. A crop is only offered in a season if ≥ 5% of districts grow it then. Sugarcane, coconut and fruit trees are split equally across the 3 seasons.

How we got here: three models

Oct 31 – Nov 3 Kaggle model RF on the Kaggle data. 98% accuracy on its own test split. Looked great. Failed on real districts: main crop in its list 7.8%, worse than 3 random crops (16%)
Nov 4 (morning) District model, year-round Same idea, but trained on real Soil Health Card soil + DES area. 28 crops. Worked, but ignored the sowing month: Jangoan got the same answer in Jan and Jun
Nov 4 (evening) → now Season model Adds season as an input and learns from area by season. In the app. Biggest single gain so far

Why Kaggle failedIts N-P-K numbers are on a different scale from real Soil Health Cards. Fed real soil, it picked chickpea first in 89% of districts. High test accuracy on clean data said nothing about the real world. That is a key story for the paper.

How good is it?

Leave-one-state-out test: train on 27 states, predict the 28th, repeat. "Main crop" = the crop with the most area that season in DES. "Popularity" = just suggest the most common crops of that season.

Putting it together

The ranker: fit first, money breaks ties

Our first idea was suitability × revenue. That made a barely-suitable fruit beat a well-suited field crop, because fruit earns about 10× per hectare. The rule we kept (notebook 07):

  • Keep crops with suitability ≥ 5%, best first, up to 3 (farmer can ask for up to 10).
  • Crops within 0.8 × the top crop's suitability are near-ties. Only these get re-ordered, by net return.
  • Net return per hectare = yield (t/ha) × 10 × price (₹/quintal) × (1 − cost share).

Explanations

SHAP: why did wheat get 0.495?

SHAP splits one prediction into pushes. It starts from the model's average for wheat across all inputs, then adds how much each of the 12 inputs pushed it up (green) or down (red). The pushes add up exactly to the score shown.

pushed uppushed downfinal suitability

The app turns this into a sentence: "Helped most by: Season, Humidity. Held back most by: Soil pH." Season dominates, which matches the model overall: season is its most important input (0.32), and soil values matter much less (0.04–0.05 each).

Be honest about

What it can't do (yet)

  • Revealed preference, not agronomy. The model learns what similar districts sow, which mixes in irrigation, markets and habit. It does not know what grows best in theory.
  • District level, not your field. It was trained on district averages. One farm's card can fall outside that range.
  • Soil from class counts. Soil Health Card files give Low/Medium/High counts, so district values are class midpoints.
  • Yield and price are not seasonal. Rabi rice gets the district's annual rice yield.
  • Known misses. Arid Rajasthan (guar and moth bean country, guar not in our 27 crops), Guntur after its 2022 split, districts whose neighbours farm differently.
  • Fruit. No CACP cost, old area data, orchard set-up years not counted.

Full list for the paper: research/paper_limitations.md. Open problems: docs/ongoing-issues.md.
